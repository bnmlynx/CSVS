Introduction to Vascular Science

Learn the fundamentals of the human vascular system — its structure, function, and common diseases — then test your knowledge with a short quiz.

1

Overview of the Vascular System

The vascular system (also called the circulatory system) is the network of blood vessels that transports blood throughout the body. It consists of three main types of vessels: arteries, which carry oxygenated blood away from the heart; veins, which return deoxygenated blood to the heart; and capillaries, the tiny vessels where gas and nutrient exchange occurs at the tissue level.

The heart acts as the central pump, driving blood through two circuits — the pulmonary circulation (heart to lungs and back) and the systemic circulation (heart to the rest of the body and back).

2

Structure of Blood Vessels

Arteries and veins share a common three-layered wall structure:

  • Tunica intima — The innermost layer, composed of a single layer of endothelial cells that provides a smooth surface for blood flow.
  • Tunica media — The middle layer, made of smooth muscle and elastic tissue. It is thicker in arteries than in veins, allowing arteries to withstand higher pressure.
  • Tunica adventitia (externa) — The outermost layer of connective tissue that anchors the vessel to surrounding structures.

Capillaries are much simpler — their walls are only one endothelial cell thick, which facilitates the diffusion of oxygen, carbon dioxide, and nutrients.

3

Key Concepts in Vascular Physiology

Several important principles govern how blood moves through the vascular system:

  • Blood pressure is the force exerted by blood against vessel walls. It is highest in arteries and drops progressively through capillaries and veins.
  • Vasoconstriction (narrowing) and vasodilation (widening) of blood vessels regulate blood flow and pressure, controlled by smooth muscle in the tunica media.
  • Veins contain one-way valves that prevent backflow and help return blood to the heart, especially from the lower extremities against gravity.
  • The aorta is the largest artery in the body, originating from the left ventricle and distributing oxygenated blood to the systemic circulation.
4

Common Vascular Diseases

Vascular diseases affect millions of people worldwide and are a leading cause of morbidity and mortality:

  • Atherosclerosis — The build-up of fatty plaques inside arterial walls, narrowing the lumen and reducing blood flow. It is a major risk factor for heart attack and stroke.
  • Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) — A blood clot that forms in a deep vein, usually in the legs. If the clot dislodges, it can travel to the lungs causing a pulmonary embolism.
  • Peripheral artery disease (PAD) — Reduced blood flow to the limbs (typically the legs) caused by narrowed arteries, leading to pain, numbness, and in severe cases, tissue death.
  • Aneurysm — An abnormal bulge in a weakened arterial wall. If it ruptures, particularly in the aorta, it can be life-threatening.
